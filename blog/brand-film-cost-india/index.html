Scene 01 · The quote problem

How much does a brand film cost in India in 2026?

By Nikunj, founder of PayoffUpdated 29 Sept 20268 min read

You've decided your brand needs a film. You ask three production houses for a quote. You get ₹2 lakh, ₹8 lakh and ₹35 lakh for what sounds like the same thing.

In 2026, a brand film in India costs anywhere from ₹50,000 to over ₹2 crore. Most D2C brands land between ₹3 lakh and ₹20 lakh for a digital-first film.

Below are the real price tiers, what pushes a quote up or down, and how to set a budget before you brief anyone. I've spent over eight years behind the camera and in the edit, and I'll tell you where the money goes.

Scene 03 · Where the money goes

What drives the cost of a brand film?

Six things decide most of the quote. A change in any one of them moves the number more than the choice of camera ever will.

  1. 1
    Script and conceptA finished script lets a producer price the job accurately. A vague brief gets padded, because everyone quotes for the worst case.
  2. 2
    Shoot daysEvery extra day pays for the crew, gear, location and food again. On TV-scale shoots, each additional day can add ₹4–10 lakh.
  3. 3
    TalentNew faces and creators are affordable. Celebrity fees range from about ₹1 lakh to ₹80 lakh and more.
  4. 4
    Locations and artTwo locations in one day cost less than two days in one location. A well-dressed set often beats an expensive location.
  5. 5
    Post-productionEditing, colour, sound, music licensing and VFX. Heavy VFX or animation can double a budget on its own.
  6. 6
    Where it will runA TV commercial needs broadcast specs and clearances. A film for Instagram and YouTube does not.

City matters too. Mumbai production rates run about 20–35% higher than Delhi NCR. (Cybertize Media)

From the set

Say a 60-second digital film is planned as one day at one location. On the morning of the shoot, the brand adds a second location across town. The crew moves, the light changes, and the day runs into overtime.

Now the "one-day" film needs a second day, and the crew, gear, food and location all get paid for again. The camera stayed the same. What changed the budget was a decision made too late.

Scene 06 · Overseas

How much does a brand film cost in the US, UK or UAE?

For global brands, the same film usually costs several times more to make in the US or UK.

US agencies quote $10,000 to $100,000+ for brand films, and Clutch reports an average agency project of about $42,000 (Vidico). A US director of photography runs $600–$2,000 a day. The average UK videographer day rate is £427 (YunoJuno, via Create & Elate).

That gap is why US, UK and UAE brands shoot in India or hire Indian crews for edit and post. You pay Indian rates for world-standard work, as long as the team has the craft.

Scene 08 · Your budget

How to set your brand film budget in 5 steps

Start from what the film has to do, not from a number. Then work backwards.

  1. Name the one job. Launch, trust, or ad fuel? One film can't do all three well.
  2. List where it will run. Platforms and aspect ratios decide your cut-downs and specs.
  3. Lock the script before you ask for final quotes. Quotes on a locked script are cheaper and easier to compare.
  4. Plan the cut-downs in the shoot. Shoot hooks, alternate lines and vertical frames on the same day.
  5. Keep 10–15% as contingency. Weather, reshoots and last-minute changes happen on every shoot.
Scene 09 · Questions

FAQ

How much does a 30-second ad film cost in India?

A 30-second digital ad usually costs ₹3–12 lakh with a professional studio. A 30-second TV commercial starts at around ₹15–20 lakh before airtime.

How long does a brand film take to make?

Most digital brand films take 3–6 weeks: 1–2 weeks of script and pre-production, 1–2 shoot days, then 1–3 weeks of edit, colour and sound.

Can AI make my brand film cheaper?

AI can cut the cost of concept frames, storyboards and some shots. It works best alongside a real shoot, not in place of the story.

Final scene

The real cost is a film nobody remembers

A ₹5 lakh film that people remember is cheaper than a ₹1 lakh film they scroll past. Budget for the story first, and the camera and crew follow from that.
